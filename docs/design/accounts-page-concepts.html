Accounts page — layout concepts

Concept 01

Account cards + per-card year strip

Kills the split between the list and the import grid: each account owns its own months. The row is no longer a thin line of buttons — it's a card with the colour swatch as the identity anchor, the transaction count as a real stat, and destructive actions hidden behind a ··· menu so Delete stops competing with the primary reading. Adding an account is a quiet ghost tile at the end, not a form shouting at the top of the page.

Concept 02

Dense ledger table — one row per account

Keeps the matrix you already have but fixes its ergonomics: the account column becomes a real sticky identity cell (swatch, name, type, count), the months become compact squares with a coverage bar, and row actions appear on hover instead of permanently occupying width. Scales to many accounts without the page growing.

Concept 03

Master–detail — list left, focused account right

Splits the two jobs the page currently blends. Left rail is navigation: pick an account. Right pane is work: rename it, recolour it, see its stats, import its months. Editing gets real room instead of an inline form squeezed into a row, and the import grid stops being twelve columns wide because it only ever shows one account.

Concept 04

Coverage timeline — months as the spine

Inverts the model: the year is the primary axis and accounts are tracks laid across it, like a Gantt or a contributions graph. One shared month header, a “today” marker, and each track a continuous bar rather than twelve separate boxes. Best for the question the current page answers worst — where are the holes in my data?
